\subsection{GEN-001: Data Scaling at Fixed Optimizer Budget}
\recordstatus{Preliminary; single-seed visual evidence.}
\evidence{W\&B screenshots, including the comparison with ordinary LoRA supplied
2026-10-23. Raw metric exports and immutable run configurations are unavailable.}

\textbf{Question.} Does more training data improve validation agreement within
the same number of optimizer updates?

\textbf{Setup.} The direct generator arms use nested pools of 4K, 8K, and 16K
examples: one, two, or four subsets per domain across math, code, STEM, and chat.
All three run for 1,500 updates with seed 42. The documented recipe uses
\texttt{lk\_hybrid}, four episodes per update, training context sizes 1--8,
rank 32, alpha 64, and an 8,192-token microbatch budget. AdamW uses learning
rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping 0.85, and 100 learning-rate
warmup updates. This scheduler warmup is distinct from bank reconstruction.
Validation uses 100 held-out rows per domain from subset 00000, grouped at
context size 4, every 250 updates including updates 0 and 1,500.
The additional curve is labeled \texttt{scale-1-lora-gseed42}; its intended
comparison is ordinary LoRA trained on the 4K pool at the same update budget.
Historical configuration artifacts are needed to verify all matched settings.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{assets/GEN-001-scaling-with-lora-2026-10-23.png}
\caption{GEN-001: supplied training-loss and domain-validation plots. The three
direct generator arms use 4K, 8K, and 16K training examples with the same
1,500-update budget. Ordinary scale-1 LoRA is included as a training baseline.
Agreement is an offline validation metric, not measured serving acceptance.
Curves are visual evidence; no raw values or uncertainty estimates are supplied.}
\label{fig:scaling}
\end{figure*}

\textbf{Observed behavior.} In Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}, generator validation
curves largely overlap across data scales. Most improvement occurs early, with
smaller gains thereafter. The ordinary LoRA curve improves more slowly and
finishes visibly below the generators on math, code, and STEM. Chat differences
are smaller, with a late dip in the scale-1 generator curve. Training loss is
noisy, while the generator trends finish below the ordinary LoRA trend.

\textbf{Working interpretation.} On this split, seed, and update budget, pool
size has a small observed effect compared with the gain from training itself.
Increasing available data fourfold does not produce a correspondingly large
validation gain in these curves. This is a fixed-update comparison: it neither
matches epochs nor guarantees equal processed tokens, compute, or wall time.
Larger pools receive less average reuse. The result therefore motivates asking
whether longer training or broader validation would reveal additional data
benefits, rather than concluding that data quantity is generally unimportant.

\textbf{Limits.} Validation rows are held out, but their subset identity is
represented in every training pool. One seed and screenshots cannot establish
statistical equivalence or a precise ranking. Generator versus LoRA differences
also include the much larger generator parameter count. These training curves
do not add an older generator row to the current serving benchmark tables.

\subsection{GEN-004: Bank Warmup and the Direct-Training Decision}
\recordstatus{Preliminary adaptation observation; bank workflow retired.}
\evidence{Adaptation screenshot supplied 2026-10-23; historical protocol recorded
in the 2026-10-07 working notes. Exact execution date and raw exports unavailable.}

\textbf{Question.} Does reconstructing previously trained bank LoRAs provide a
useful lasting advantage over training a fresh generator on the original data?

\textbf{Setup.} The historical study adapts four initializations on
\texttt{math-00001}: a bank-pretrained generator, a fresh generator, a transferred
ordinary LoRA, and a fresh ordinary LoRA. The recorded protocol uses seed 42,
last-token conditioning, training context sizes 1--8, and one adaptation epoch.
The supplied plot spans roughly 200 optimizer updates and evaluates context 8;
this is distinct from GEN-001's context-4 validation. Both generator arms are
optimized through the drafter loss during adaptation.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.70\textwidth]{assets/GEN-004-adaptation-2026-10-23.png}
\caption{GEN-004: supplied adaptation curves for pretrained/fresh generators and
transferred/fresh ordinary LoRAs. Bank pretraining improves the starting point,
but the fresh generator reaches a similar final context-8 validation loss.
The displayed comparison does not match total pretraining-plus-adaptation cost.}
\label{fig:adaptation}
\end{figure*}

\textbf{Observed behavior.} The pretrained generator starts at a lower
validation loss than the fresh generator. By the end of the displayed adaptation,
the two generator validation curves meet at a similar loss. The transferred
ordinary LoRA changes little, while the fresh ordinary LoRA improves but remains
above the generators. Figure~\ref{fig:adaptation} therefore shows an initialization
benefit from bank warmup without an observed final-loss advantage at this budget.

\textbf{Design decision.} We retired the LoRA-bank approach as unhelpful for the
current workflow. We use the original examples directly to train the generator
with the drafting loss. Bank reconstruction first turns those examples into
trained LoRA factors and then asks the generator to reproduce that proxy.
Direct training optimizes the desired drafting objective itself and removes
both bank construction and proxy reconstruction from the workflow. The observed
fresh-generator catch-up supports choosing this simpler route; no further bank
experiments are part of the current plan.

\textbf{Scope of the claim.} Direct training is the chosen, more useful approach
for this project, rather than a demonstrated universal ordering of the methods.
This adaptation study is not a matched-total-budget comparison of using the same
examples for bank warmup versus direct pretraining. It also mixes conditioning
and parameter-capacity differences between generators and ordinary LoRA. Raw
metrics, repeated seeds, and complete historical provenance are missing.
We retain the new screenshot without reusing approximate endpoint values from
older screenshots in the working notes.

\subsection{GEN-002: Output Repeatability Investigation}
\recordstatus{Investigation deferred; exact sequence comparisons recorded.}
\evidence{Local correctness reports summarized in the Markdown registry,
observation dated 2026-10-21. Raw reports are not archived with this report.}

\textbf{Question.} Were target-only and speculative outputs repeatable under
the supplied prompt/settings fingerprints?

On 50 Nemotron math prompts, original versus repeated target-only decoding
matched exactly for 23/50 sequences; original versus repeated EAGLE3 decoding
matched for 50/50. Repeated target-only versus repeated EAGLE3 matched for
17/50. These are whole-sequence match counts, not token-error rates.

\textbf{Record notes.} The cause remains unresolved. Runtime environment,
numerical behavior, and decoding implementation differences have not been
isolated. Current timing experiments explicitly record correctness as unchecked
or unverified. No correctness claim is inferred from the throughput tables.

\textbf{Missing evidence.} Preserve and compare complete runtime provenance and
repeat a controlled reference experiment before making an exact-output claim.

\recordstatus{Implemented; GPU measurements pending.}
\evidence{Implementation revision \texttt{c0ee0e4}; CPU lifecycle and HTTP
tests, plus an audit of the pinned vLLM hook upgrade.}

\textbf{Question.} What measurements arise when a generator conditions one
shared adapter on a fixed group at concurrency 1, 2, 4, or 8?

\textbf{Planned measurement configuration.} Reuse the frozen category prompts
for all model/concurrency variants. Record generated output tokens/s,
speculative acceptance where available, request latency, actual mean batch size,

\textbf{Available checks.} Tests cover packed prompt boundaries, one generator
invocation per group, restoration after the last member, delayed HTTP cleanup,
closed admission, partial groups, and suite resume across concurrency variants.
The initial broader CPU/HTTP run passed 94 tests; a later focused run after
controller refinements passed 50. These runs overlap and are not additive.

\textbf{Pending evidence.} No A100 execution, batched throughput table,

neither GPU compatibility under load nor a performance benefit.
